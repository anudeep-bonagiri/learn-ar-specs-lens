# Council Round 1: all perspectives

## Snap Specs Platform & DevRel Lead
### Position
Snap should want **Loci, rebuilt as an AI-generated memory palace that people use again and again**. It is the only candidate where you walk your own home with your hands free while the content sits in the room. Land It should go. Smoke Line is worthy, but it's a one-time-use product Snap would not put on a launch reel.

### Key points
- **(a) What Snap wants:**
  1. A 5 to 10 second moment for a launch reel, where a phone holder would look silly doing the same thing.
  2. At least three platform kits clearly visible: SIK pinch/grab, UI Kit, spatial sound, and ideally an AI call (Gemini/ChatGPT) with a hard-coded fallback.
  3. A reason to open it again tomorrow, such as a streak or a review schedule.
  4. The CLAD story on screen: the design brief, LEAF tests going green, and a few seconds of the agent building through the MCP.
  5. Content at 0.6 to 1.6 m and few big targets.
- **(b) Loci: likely winner, upgrade it.** Its key improvement is to have AI generate 6 to 8 cards with 3D icons from any topic, with a hard-coded fallback. Add a review card ("revisit tomorrow, streak 1") at the end.
- **(b) Smoke Line: good cause, wrong showcase.** It's a once-a-year use and a fire in a home is not a launch-marketing asset. Kidde's version exists, and "press Q to crouch" looks weak. Its key improvement is to make it a family drill a parent runs for a kid, with directional alarm audio.
- **(b) Land It: replace it.** It is a tech demo that fails the phone test and has no retention.
- **(c) NEW, Cue Walk.** Paste your talk notes, AI splits them into beats, and you pin each beat to a spot in the room. You rehearse walking the route. A beat glows only when you face it, a chime leads you on, and a pacing ring shows time per beat. Uses W and B. A phone fails because rehearsing a talk while holding a phone defeats the purpose. Stand-ins: a pinch "next" button replaces voice, and a hard-coded talk replaces AI chunking.
- **(c) NEW, Fix It.** A hands-free repair guide, such as a P-trap or bike wheel. Step labels and ghost hands sit on the part, a pinch on "done" advances, and a "missing washer?" Commerce Kit card closes the loop. Uses W and B. Its risk is reading as "the risotto coach for plumbing".
- **Recommendation:** Loci with AI decks as the engine and Cue Walk as the headline scenario. End the video with a whole-room view of the lit route.

### Risks & blind spots
- AI in the editor is unreliable, so the fallback must be the video's default path.
- Memory-palace prior art means the pitch must name what's new.
- Retention can't be demoed, so mark it as roadmap.
- Brand safety: fire in a home with kids is a problem for Snap.
- Commerce Kit adoption matters to Snap.
- CLAD use must be shown on screen, not just claimed.
- Walking in the WASD preview is slow, so use 5 or 6 stations.

### Confidence
medium: confident about what Snap's business wants, but unsure how judges weigh it against polish, and prior art for Cue Walk and Fix It is unchecked.

## Devpost Learn Curriculum Lead
### Position
Land It should win, rebuilt as a predict-observe-explain loop ("Call the Shot"). It needs no stand-ins, every criterion is logic-provable, and a Lens that visibly teaches is the best case study for the series. Loci is a strong runner-up if tightened. Smoke Line has the best pitch but the weakest proof in preview, so it should be cut to one moment.

### Key points
- **(a) What Devpost Learn needs. Each item is checkable:**
  1. The brief names one problem, one audience and one moment, plus a "can't prove in preview" list.
  2. There are 3 to 6 done criteria, each visible in preview.
  3. Each criterion has a LEAF test, and passing runs appear in the repo and in the video.
  4. A design-notes log records at least 2 iterations, each with a reason.
  5. A table labels claims logic-proven or feel-unproven.
  6. Stand-ins are named on screen.
  7. The video is under 3 minutes and shows the full loop with no hiding cuts.
- **(b) Land It: build it as a lesson.** Its best test is the engine's landing point matching the projectile formula within tolerance. Its key improvement is a predict → drop → explain loop around one concept ("why 45° isn't the max off a ramp"), run over 3 rounds, ending on a prediction-error chart that shrinks.
- **(b) Loci is a strong runner-up.** Its key improvement is to fix scope to one session and give the deck a real job, such as a 6-point talk outline. Label "improves memory" feel-unproven and never claim it.
- **(b) Smoke Line: cut it to "Low and Out".** Drop PASS, since pinch-and-drag mis-teaches a motor skill. LEAF asserts exposure accumulates only while head height is above the smoke layer.
- **(c) Call the Shot.** Set the ramp angle, walk across the room and pinch where you think the ball lands. The arc stays drawn at 1:1 scale beside your guess. Uses W and B, with no stand-ins.
- **(c) Talk Track.** Place 6 outline cards along a loop and rehearse out loud while walking. Orbs light by proximity, and a pinch reveals a card only if you stall.
- **Don't merge.** Every merge adds a second spatial moment.
- **Selling the series.** A winner whose repo shows the full process becomes the next skill-pack worked example, replacing the risotto coach.

### Risks & blind spots
- This lens favors what can be proven over consumer appeal. Call the Shot is a weak reason to buy Specs.
- Phone AR can show arcs, so the video must show real scale and walking to the prediction.
- Feel-unproven labels can cost Presentation points unless framed as rigor.
- Physics vs formula disagreement (friction, timestep) can make the flagship test flaky.
- Ray-placement drift in Loci means tests should check stored positions.
- The brief must be stable by the Nov 5 review.

### Confidence
medium.

## Spectacles Design Judge
### Position
Back a merged Smoke Line + Loci escape drill, "Lights Out", where cues fade run by run until the last run is done from memory, under smoke, unaided. Land It is the weakest, so rebuild it as a walk-around throw ("Arc Freeze") or cut it.

### Key points
- **(a) What I'd check in the video:**
  - One continuous run through a complete loop: setup, task, feedback, end score, and a reason to replay.
  - At least one shot where the camera walks around world-anchored content.
  - At least one moment where hands or body do real work.
  - A "why not a phone" line in the first 30 seconds.
  - 10 to 15 seconds of CLAD building through the MCP and LEAF tests going green.
  - Stand-ins labeled honestly.
  - Design habits shown on screen.
- **(b) Smoke Line is the strongest but needs focus.** It passes the phone test and uses W and B. Kidde and Proximity Pulse echo it. The fix is to cut PASS to a single pinch or drop it, and invest in the crouch-and-escape plus runs that teach.
- **(b) Loci is a good fit for Learn, with a weak hook.** Text on orbs is flashcards with walking. The fix is to anchor a vivid, absurd 3D image on a specific piece of furniture, which is how the method works, and show forgetting within the session with a 30-second distractor before recall.
- **(b) Land It uses no strength beyond a light W.** PhET exists. Make the user the launcher or drop it.
- **(c) Lights Out.** Run 1 has full floor arrows, run 2 has orbs only at decision points, and run 3 has nothing. Smoke drops, you crouch, find the door from memory, and get scored on time and wrong turns. It ends with "you don't need me anymore." This is AR designed to remove itself.
- **(c) Arc Freeze.** Pinch a predicted ring, then throw the ball with SIK. The arc freezes at 1:1 with velocity vectors, you walk around it, and a slider scrubs time. Almost no stand-ins.
- **Don't go AI-led.** Decisionator, Fireside Tales, Noodle and AI Teleport have already used that up.

### Risks & blind spots
- The additive display means smoke can only add light haze, so make it a bright "danger layer" with a hard edge, and make the floor cues bright.
- Crouch is invisible in preview, so add a height gauge and a sound and color change when crossing the line.
- The preview room is generic, so pitch "set up once, rehearse forever."
- Fire drills carry liability, so frame it as a rehearsal aid.
- "Shares your view" goes unused.
- Scope: polish run 3 first.

### Confidence
medium.

## Skeptical Specs Buyer
### Position
None of the three candidates gives a $2,195 buyer a reason to buy. Loci is a niche study trick, Smoke Line is once a year, and Land It is a classroom sim. The winner should be a job people already do with their hands busy and their eyes on the task, where pulling out a phone is the real friction.

### Key points
- **(a) What a buyer, and so Snap, needs:**
  1. In the first 15 seconds the video names a real chore or task.
  2. Within the first minute it shows the phone version failing: wet or dirty hands, both hands full, or eyes off the work.
  3. There are at least two reasons to come back, so one fixed script doesn't count.
  4. Nothing embarrassing to do in front of a roommate, like arm-waving or crouching.
  5. The pitch ends with "I'd show my friend this".
- **(b) Loci is niche.** MemoryVaultAR is already on iPad, and a hard-coded deck kills reuse. Its key improvement is to tie it to a dreaded memorization job, such as a talk, toast, audition or exam formulas, with user-entered items.
- **(b) Smoke Line is one-and-done.** The crouch is a feel factor, and pressing Q looks like a joke on video. Its key improvement is to drop PASS and keep a timed escape through your own layout, pitched as "a 6-year-old learns their real exit route."
- **(b) Land It is the weakest.** PhET already does this for free. If kept, it should become a trick-shot game with levels. The verdict is to replace it.
- **(c) NEW, Flatpack.** An assembly coach where a glowing ghost of the next part slides into its exact spot on the half-built piece. A big "done" pinch advances, and a counted screw tray floats near your hand. It uses W, B and S (sending your view to a helper). A phone fails because both hands hold a panel and an Allen key. Stand-ins are a "found it" pinch and a shelf model on the table. It must lead with the ghost part snapping in, not a checklist, or it looks like the cooking coaches.
- **(c) NEW, Walk the Talk.** Place the beats of a talk or toast around the room, then walk and speak. The next keyword floats slightly below where you look, a pace ring fills, and an end card shows which beats needed a peek. A phone fails because holding notes is the habit you're breaking.
- **(c) NEW, Smoke Line Home.** A parent marks exits, the shutoff valve and the breaker panel. The child runs a timed drill, and the floor arrows fade over repeat runs.

### Risks & blind spots
- Preview hides comfort, so long sessions (45-minute assembly) or crouching will hurt real buyers.
- "Educational" is where retention dies. Card Master and CPR worked because they had stakes or an opponent.
- Social embarrassment: pacing your living room talking to orbs is awkward, while assembly and rehearsal are things you already do visibly.
- Step coaches risk looking generic next to the cooking crowd.
- "What impresses sponsors" and "what makes a buyer pay" are the same thing here.

### Confidence
medium.

## Devil's Advocate
### Position
All three candidates assume judges will picture your real home. They won't: they watch a simulated room with a virtual table. The winner should lose nothing in preview, so pick Arc (a reworked Land It), with Fault Line as the emotional replacement for Smoke Line.

### Key points
- **(a) What can actually be checked:**
  1. Every second of the video is the real Lens, with no "imagine this at home."
  2. The toolchain is visibly proven: the brief, CLAD driving the MCP, and green LEAF tests, shown for 15 to 20 seconds.
  3. A one-sentence phone-test reason.
  "Consumer reason to buy" is a trap in a 3-week contest. HoloATC and Wi-Fi Speed won on one clear spatial trick.
- **(b) Loci: reject as written.** The method needs persistence, which can't be shown. The preview room is a few metres, so the "route" is three orbs a step apart, and it looks like pinching orbs to flip cards. If kept, make it one session where the room's own objects are the hooks, and test recall from another spot.
- **(b) Smoke Line: best pitch, worst demo.** Additive smoke is faint glow, the crouch is the Q key, and Kidde exists. Wearing $2,195 glasses that narrow your view in a fire is a credibility hole. Either make PASS the hero or swap to Fault Line.
- **(b) Land It is the most honest, but tapping a prediction works in phone AR.** The fix is to draw the prediction in the world.
- **(c) Arc, my pick.** Pinch-drag in mid-air to trace the 3D path you think the ball will fly. Your glowing line stays, the real path draws beside it, and you walk around to see whether you misjudged height or distance. Level 2 adds a cup in an awkward spot. Uses W and B. A phone can't trace a free 3D curve at real scale or let you walk around it. No stand-ins, plus an optional AI coach line with a fallback.
- **(c) Fault Line.** An earthquake drill around the preview's own virtual table. Objects topple with physics, spatial sound comes from where things fall, and a lamp swings. You drop, take cover under the table and hold the leg, then get judged on whether you reached cover before the impact zone. A top-down replay shows what would have hit you. The preview's biggest limit, its fixed virtual table, becomes the hero object.
- **Challenge: don't avoid AI entirely.** Snap OS 2.0's headline is AI Lenses, so add a one-day scoped AI moment with a fallback.
- **Don't overcorrect away from hands-busy tasks.**

### Risks & blind spots
- Judges watch first and imagine second.
- The additive display kills dark visuals.
- Arc needs a real wrapper (3 escalating challenges, a score, a replay, an audience) or it is a toy.
- Retention could come from a daily target.
- Prior art for earthquake VR sims (ShakeOut) and mid-air trajectory sketching is unchecked.
- Drill Lenses carry liability optics, so frame them as rehearsal.

### Confidence
medium.
