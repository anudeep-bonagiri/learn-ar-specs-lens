# Council debate: what the companies want to see

Run 2026-10-16 with [hex/claude-council](https://github.com/hex/claude-council) in local council mode, with two debate rounds.
No outside model providers were configured, so every member is Claude playing a stakeholder role.

> **Local council.** These perspectives all come from Claude playing different roles, not from different AI vendors.
> Treat agreement as a shared starting point to pressure-test, not as independent confirmation.

**Members:**
- Snap Specs Platform & DevRel Lead
- Devpost Learn Curriculum Lead
- Spectacles Design Judge
- Skeptical $2,195 Specs Buyer
- Devil's Advocate

The four stakeholder roles were custom-written for this question. The Devil's Advocate is the plugin's built-in role.

**Format:**
- Round 1: blind, independent answers.
- Round 2: each member read all five answers, rebutted, and named one final pick.

## What each company wants, in checkable terms

**Snap**, the sponsor and the platform:
- A 5 to 10 second clip it could put in a Specs launch reel, where holding a phone would look silly.
- A consumer reason to wear $2,195 glasses more than once. Lenses can't be "used once a year."
- Visible platform kits: SIK, UI Kit, spatial sound, and ideally one AI step that does real work, with a fallback.
- Proof that agentic development works: about 15 seconds of CLAD building through the Lens Studio MCP, with LEAF tests going green, in the video.
- Brand safety. A fire or disaster in a family home is a hard sell for launch marketing.

**Devpost Learn**, the host:
- Proof the format teaches. The repo should show each step:
  - A brief with one problem, one audience and one moment.
  - 3 to 6 done criteria visible in preview.
  - A passing LEAF test for each.
  - A design-notes log with at least 2 iterations, each with its reason.
  - A logic-proven vs. feel-unproven table.
  - Every stand-in labeled on screen.
- A winner that can become the next skill-pack worked example, replacing the risotto coach.

**Judges**, scoring the video:
- One continuous loop: setup, task, feedback, an end state, and a reason to replay.
- A shot where the camera walks around world-anchored content.
- A moment where the body does real work.
- A "why not a phone" line in the first 30 seconds.
- No "imagine this in your home" voiceover. Judges watch first and imagine second.

**A buyer:**
- A real, dreaded task named in the first 15 seconds.
- The phone version visibly failing.
- A reason to come back.
- Nothing embarrassing to do in front of a roommate.

## Strongest criticisms that survived the debate

- **AI as the headline is a trap in preview.** The fallback becomes what the video shows, so the claimed feature never appears. Decisionator, Fireside Tales, Noodle and AI Teleport have already used "AI makes the content."
- **Persistence claims ("streak", "tomorrow") are theater.** Persistence is on the can't-show list.
- **The preview room is small and generic.** A memory "route" is three orbs a step apart, and "find the exit from memory" is trivial with one visible door.
- **The additive display can't draw darkness.** Smoke reads as faint glow, not danger.
- **The Q-key crouch reads as the camera sliding down.** It is a feel factor.
- **Anything that needs recognition becomes virtual-on-virtual.** That covers Flatpack and the ghost parts.
- **Three of five members independently proposed talk rehearsal.** Expect other entrants to land there too.
- **Tapping a landing guess is the phone-AR version.** The 3D-ness has to come through walking and camera movement.

## Synthesis

**Shared starting points, to stress-test rather than trust:**
- Every member converged on a few things:
  - Use one loop with escalating runs.
  - Show the toolchain on screen.
  - Name the phone test early.
  - Never fake the headline feature.
- Every member also converged on the **fading cues across runs** mechanic. The Design Judge introduced it, and three others adopted it.
- Since all members are the same model, ask what they might all miss. The likeliest blind spot is **taste**. None of them can know how this specific panel weighs consumer appeal against process rigor.

**The real tension is "what Snap can market" versus "what the preview can prove."**
- **Walk the Talk** wins on consumer pull, retention and a launch-reel shot. Its core action, speaking out loud, can only be shown as a button plus voiceover.
- **Call the Shot** loses nothing in preview. It has the best LEAF test in the contest, comparing the physics engine to the projectile formula. But two members call it a physics toy with no reason to buy Specs.
- **Fault Line** has the most cinematic shot, the frozen debris you walk around. It still relies on the Q-crouch, which three members flagged, and it carries disaster-marketing optics.

**Blind spots no member fully covered:**
- **Who the judges are.** The rules name criteria, not the panel. That premise decides the split, and no member could check it.
- **Audio in the video.** A screen recording can carry your real voice even though the preview can't hear it. That rescues Walk the Talk's core action on camera, as long as the Lens works without voice.
- **Prior art, checked after the debate:**
  - AR glasses speech rehearsal exists only as research prototypes, such as Ring-AR Presenter and SpeakAR. Shipping cue-card apps are phone and tablet only.
  - AR earthquake drills exist as a 2018 Berkeley student project and a 2021 research paper.
  - AR and VR projectile labs exist on HoloLens and Quest.
  - Kids' fire drills with fading cues exist only as VR studies, with no AR version.

**Suggested direction: build Walk the Talk, engineered with Call the Shot's discipline.**
- It is the only finalist that satisfies Snap, the sponsor, and the buyer test.
- The Snap Platform Lead and the Buyer back it. Even its critics' objections are fixable in presentation rather than fatal:
  - **"It's a teleprompter."** A teleprompter shows everything all the time. This hides the notes, fades them, and measures recall in peeks. Lead with that difference.
  - **"Voice is a button."** Speak the talk for real over the screen recording. Pinching "next" stays the input, and the voiceover is the proof.
  - **"Others will build it too."** The difference is in the details: the fade, cues placed on furniture that appear only when you look at them, a peek count, and a spatial chime pulling you to the next beat. A plain "notes floating in the room" entry won't have them.
- **Real uncertainty remains** on whether a design panel finds it innovative enough. Settle it cheaply: ask in #specs-general or at the Oct 22 build session how the judges weigh consumer use against craft.
- **Backup:** if they say craft and provability, switch to Call the Shot before Nov 1.

## Final concept: Walk the Talk

**Pitch:** "Rehearse your toast or talk by walking it around your room. Your notes live on the furniture and fade every run, until you give it with none."

**Audience:** anyone with a dreaded speech coming up, such as a best-man toast, a class presentation, a job-interview pitch or a sales demo.
**Hook line for the first 15 seconds:** "Everyone rehearses by pacing the room. Everyone holds their notes. That's the habit that makes you read instead of talk."

### The one spatial moment
It's run 3. You walk the route with nothing showing, and you stall at the bookshelf.
Only then does that beat's icon glow faintly, just below where you're looking. You recover, and a chime from the direction of the window pulls you to the next beat.
The end card reads "0 peeks · 2:41 · no notes."

### Strengths
- **Draws in the world:** beats are anchored on real furniture, and cues appear where your eyes already go.
- **Leaves your body free:** hands empty and eyes up while you walk and speak.
- **Shares your view (roadmap only):** send the end card or your view to a friend for feedback.

### Why a phone can't
Holding notes is the exact habit rehearsal is meant to break. A phone pulls your eyes down and away from your imagined audience.

### Loop
1. **Load a talk.** Use the hard-coded best-man toast, or a second sample talk. An optional AI step splits pasted notes into 5 beats, with the hard-coded talk as the fallback, labeled on screen. AI cuts *your* text into beats and never invents content.
2. **Place.** Pinch 5 anchors on the preview room's furniture: table, shelf, window, door, lamp.
3. **Run 1** shows full sentences. **Run 2** shows one keyword. **Run 3** shows an icon only.
4. **Peek** by pinching to see the full text. Each peek is counted.
5. **End card** shows peeks per beat, time per beat, and the change from run 1 to run 3. A "New talk" button loads the second talk to show it is reusable.

### Where things live
- Beat orbs sit on furniture anchors 0.6 to 1.6 m from the user, about 6 cm across.
- Cue text sits just below the gaze line, in light text on a pale panel, never black, because the display is additive.
- A pace ring of about 8 cm surrounds the active orb.
- The end card is 40 × 25 cm, 1 m away, slightly below eye level.

### Done criteria

Each has a LEAF test and can be checked in preview.

1. Five beats load, from AI or the fallback, and pinch-placing stores 5 world positions 0.6 to 1.6 m from the user.
2. A beat's cue shows only when the user is within 2 m and facing it within a 15° cone. Every other beat stays a dim orb.
3. Cue content fades by run: a sentence, then a keyword, then an icon. A peek pinch shows the full text and adds 1 to that beat's peek count.
4. The pace ring fills against each beat's target time and turns amber when over. A spatial chime plays from the next beat's position.
5. The end card shows peeks and time per beat for runs 1 to 3, and "New talk" loads the second talk.

### Stand-ins, labeled on screen
- A "next beat" pinch replaces voice and pace detection.
- The hard-coded talk replaces AI chunking whenever AI fails.
- The preview room's furniture replaces your living room.

### Out of scope
Saving talks across sessions, real speech recognition, audience avatars, and multi-user feedback.

### Video plan, under 3 minutes

| Time | Shot |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:15 | The hook line plus a real, dreaded moment: "Best-man toast. Saturday. Can't hold my phone." |
| 0:15–0:40 | Place 5 beats on the furniture, with the camera walking around them. |
| 0:40–1:05 | Run 1 with full text. Speak the toast for real over the recording. |
| 1:05–1:20 | CLAD building it through the Lens Studio MCP, then five LEAF tests going green. |
| 1:20–1:45 | Run 2 with keywords only and one peek. |
| 1:45–2:30 | Run 3 with icons only: the stall, the glow, the chime, then "0 peeks." |
| 2:30–3:00 | End card showing runs 1 to 3, the logic-proven and feel-unproven labels, and why glasses. |

### Process artifacts Devpost wants in the repo
- The approved `devpost/design-brief.md`.
- LEAF results for each criterion.
- `devpost/design-notes.md` with at least 2 dated iterations and their reasons.
- A logic-proven vs. feel-unproven table in the README.

### Biggest risk
Walking and pinching orbs can look like a tech demo if the voiceover is missing or flat. The real spoken toast over the recording carries the whole Presentation score.

## Runners-up, as the council refined them

### Call the Shot (backup if judges weigh provability)
- **Pitch:** "Walk to where you think the ball lands, then walk around the frozen life-size arc to see why you were wrong."
- **Moment:** set the ramp angle with a slider and stand where you think it lands. The ball launches, and its real path freezes at 1:1 with velocity arrows. You walk under its peak and scrub a time slider.
- **Done criteria:**
  1. A deterministic launch lands within 2 cm of the projectile formula. This is the flagship LEAF test.
  2. A pinch records your guess, and the error shows in cm.
  3. The arc stays world-anchored after the camera moves 1.5 m.
  4. Three rounds run: open floor, a cup, then a cup behind a box.
  5. An end chart shows the error shrinking.
- **Risk:** it reads as a 3D PhET simulation unless the three-round lesson is polished.

### Fault Line (most cinematic)
- **Pitch:** "An earthquake rehearsal around your table. Drop, take cover, hold on, then walk through the frozen moment to see what would have hit you."
- **Moment:** objects topple with spatial sound and you get under the table. Everything freezes mid-fall, and you walk around the debris and a ghost of yourself.
- **Done criteria:**
  1. At least 4 physics objects fall, each with spatial audio.
  2. "Covered" requires the camera below and inside the tabletop's footprint.
  3. Cues fade over 3 runs.
  4. Securing 3 hazards stops them falling.
  5. The replay flags landings within 0.5 m of your head.
- **Risks:**
  - The Q-key drop must read clearly. Add a height indicator.
  - It only matters in quake zones.
  - ShakeOut-style VR drills are prior art.
